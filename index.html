Full-payment discount or a 50/50 plan

Two apartments, both handed over in a year, both worth 2,000 AED per sqft on exit. The first is priced at 1,224 AED/sqft, but the money is due in full today. The second is 1,664 AED/sqft with the payment split in half. The question is not which one makes more profit, but which one earns more on the capital actually tied up, and for how long.

What if the market never reaches 2,000

Both models rest on a single assumption — the exit price. Option B is the more sensitive of the two: only half as much of your own money is in the deal, so a drop in price hits the return twice as hard.

Exit price IRR A IRR B Profit A Profit B

How this is calculated. IRR is the rate at which the present value of all payments equals zero. Option A has two flows: the price out today, the proceeds in at handover. Option B has three: half out today, half out at handover, proceeds in. When the sale lands exactly on the handover date, the second instalment and the proceeds collapse into a single flow — which is why option B looks so strong at a low price: only half the capital is ever committed.

Costs. The 4% DLD fee is charged on the full contract price and is paid immediately at Oqood registration — in option B it goes out on the day of the first instalment, even though only half the apartment has been paid for. Commission on resale is 2%. The toggle switches both off if you want the raw arithmetic.

What the model does not know. IRR does not price risk. Option A puts the entire capital with the developer for a year; option B leaves half the money with the buyer and keeps the door open to exit by assignment without ever making the second payment. At equal returns these are still different deals. Per the DLD registry the project is at 0.00% completion, last inspected on 2 September 2025.

The spread in the price list. 1,224 against 1,664 per sqft is a 26.4% discount for giving up the payment plan. Break-even sits at 1,472, i.e. at a discount of roughly 17%: everything the developer gives beyond that is the pure gain of paying in full. Here it is given with almost ten percentage points to spare.
